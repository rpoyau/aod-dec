## Findings and application consequences

**C01 — Chirality tag and flip are defined; a probability law is additional.**
Main `sections/05_curl_closure_duon_current.tex`,
`eq:hinge-chirality-tag` and `eq:hinge-chirality-flip`, defines the handed
branch order by `chi=+1` for Alpha–hinge–Omega and `chi=-1` for its exchanged
order. The flip is the exchange of those complete presentations. The local
ternary role `sigma in {-1,0,+1}` is a different type: the binary chirality
selection does not remove the mandatory hinge or license direct branch bypass.

The owner's new probability can therefore be declared on whole-return
orientation/recipe selection. At fixed context and rational `0<=q<=1`, the
two-state transition kernel is `[[1-q,q],[q,1-q]]`. This is a chosen symmetric
flip law, not an extracted necessity of the source definition. A different
direction-dependent pair of probabilities would be another declared law.

**C02 — The source permits Markov continuity but does not infer it from a
projection.** Main `sections/06_wave_state_relational_time.tex`,
`subsec:duon-continuity`, explicitly distinguishes Markovian present-cycle
continuity from retained-history continuity. `eq:complete-wave-state`,
`eq:wave-update-trace`, and `eq:coupling-trace` retain the actual prerequisites
needed by the declared update. Its `eq:admitted-kernel` selects rational weights
on already admitted support; it does not admit a new wave or closure.

Manual `06_field_dynamics_applications.tex:19–27` requires a reduction to declare
its discarded state and test future sufficiency. If flip probability depends
on position/core membership, chirality alone generally omits a transition
operand. A full state such as `(position_A,chi_A,position_B,chi_B)` can support
the declared Markov law when all additional probability inputs are fixed or
included in that state. If an update uses previous contact, pressure, lag or
RCD, retain those operands too. Complete logs can coexist with a Markov law:
retention does not force history dependence, and aggregate equality does not
prove its absence.

**C03 — A field core needs a declared support map.** Main
`eq:field-organization` resolves fields to distinct admitted recurring members;
aliases do not add population. `subsec:medium-declaration-field-count` declares
candidate/support counts, raw capacity and window operands and bounds actual
admitted membership. Neither that construction nor bare tesseract incidence
selects a unique geometric core. The eight cubic facets of an unweighted
tesseract shell are symmetry-related.

Declare a core cell set or a source-bound population/support profile before
assigning `q_core>q_shell`. A fixed tagged core is a valid finite application
choice; it is not a metric centre inferred from topology. Population per cell
is not SI density without a measure. Do not reuse `rho^D` for population
density: it remains the duration projection of directed RCD.

**C04 — A flip, an overlap, coupling and fusion are distinct claims.** Manual
`manual:orbital-retention-field-dynamics-fixture` defines
`FlipClass in {early_coupling,fizz,pending,sheddic_route}` as an application
classifier. Its early-coupling predicate consumes positive duration
participation, a capacity comparison, phase and impedance gates, and nonzero
attention change. It is not merely `flip && in_core`. Fizz names unresolved
successor/background motion; pending and sheddic states retain their own
conditions and route evidence.

If those operands are absent, report a sampled flip and declared overlap/contact
candidate without promoting it to early coupling or fusion. Matter I
`manual2:folding-time` explicitly says neither common endpoints nor handedness
flip supplies an outer certificate. Its fusion definition still requires
admitted operands, mapped inner histories, and an independently closed outer
proposal.

`manual2/sections/10-all-element-campaign.tex`,
`eq:manual2-campaign-orientation`, declares weights
`w_i^±=1+max(0,±R_i)` and normalized `P_i^±`. It then executes the selected
complete continuation recipe through H1 traversals, hinge and return, and
independently certifies it. This is distinct from claiming two alternatives at

The new core-biased rule may replace that recipe-selection model only in its

**C06 — Proton-facing support is not yet a physical collision event model.**
The Matter I campaign constructs proton-facing `3:3`, neutron-facing `3:4`,
and electron-facing `1:2` recurrent supports with actual formation witnesses.
It explicitly leaves physical electronic/nuclear preparation correspondence
as an additional obligation. `manual2:paired-configurations` likewise keeps
physical preparation and nuclear couplings unspecified. The inspected sources
do not equate a proton–proton collision with a chirality flip or provide a
core-biased pp scattering probability.

A physical comparison would need a defined incoming preparation and collision
energy, state/interaction-to-observable map, quantities and units, and a matching
measured target. For context only, the primary
[ATLAS elastic-scattering account](https://atlas.cern/Updates/Physics-Briefing/ALFA-scattering)
reports pp events through reconstructed momenta/scattering angle, momentum
transfer, collision energy and cross section. This source is not an input to
the proposed native transition kernel. The finite example should be called
collision-inspired/core-biased chiral transport unless those additional maps
and targets are actually supplied.

## Minimal declared experiment and falsifiers

1. Keep existing incidence immutable. Declare the position-support map,
   adjacency/movement kernel, core set, update granularity, initial pair state,
   and exact rational `q_shell`, `q_core`. State whether q uses pre-move or
   post-move positions; retain the choice in every record.
2. If the two flip draws are conditionally independent, declare that factorization.
   It is not implied by calling the collision Markovian. A correlated pair law
   requires an explicit four-outcome joint kernel.
3. Carry immutable actor/lineage identifiers, old/new positions and chirality,
   scope/cut, selected probability, random draw, and predecessor event identity.
   Retain both inputs when signed current cancels. Probability one, topology and
   replay checks should be exact.
4. Compare the exact law or expectations with an independently implemented
   enumerator and retain sampled runs separately. Report flips/opportunities
   conditioned on core/shell exposure, not raw totals alone: different residence
   times confound raw counts. A larger core propensity need not make every
   realized sample or final odd-parity probability larger.
5. Use controls `q=0`, `q=1`, equal core/shell probabilities, and a fixed-position
   fixed-q closed form:
   `P(chi_n != chi_0) = [1-(1-2q)^n]/2` and `E[number_of_flips]=nq`.
   For two independent equal-q draws, probability of exactly one flip is
   `2q(1-q)`. Core relabelling with all inputs transformed should preserve the
   corresponding result. Hidden-position/history projections require their own
   sufficiency test.
6. Keep probability fractions, integer event/bin residuals, sample diagnostics,
   numerical display rounding, model discrepancy and unknown physical mapping
   separate. Do not reuse the old aggregate solver percentage as collision
   accuracy or as a cross section.
